Ordinary Mac activity unrelated to a Decision 0009 evidence premise may continue normally.

### 2. Protected-location cleanup prohibition

During an active Decision 0009 preservation period, no discretionary or automated cleanup may delete, move, rename, replace, recreate, truncate, or otherwise alter:

- P;
- I;
- the eventual O;
- Q once created;
- any required parent or ancestor namespace on which their qualification depends.

No new cleanup mechanism, rule, job, service, policy, or equivalent automated process may be introduced that can reach those locations without explicit Human Chair authorization and appropriate evidence review.

The prohibition includes recreation or replacement of a directory under the same pathname where the resulting filesystem object would have a different identity.

A pathname that appears unchanged does not, by itself, establish preservation of the qualified object or namespace.

### 3. Security-first rule

Decision 0009 must not require postponement of a necessary security patch, emergency security update, or urgent cybersecurity remediation.

Security patches and other reasonably necessary security remediation may be applied promptly during the preservation period.

During an active cybersecurity incident or elevated threat period, protection of the host takes priority over convenience or uninterrupted progress of Decision 0009.

Decision 0009 work may pause at a safe point while urgent remediation occurs.

Application of a security update does not itself constitute a Decision 0009 violation and does not automatically invalidate preserved artifacts or unrelated governance decisions.

If the security action materially affects a technical premise on which a qualification relies, only the affected qualification is suspended until the relevant premise is re-established.

Unaffected evidence and governance remain valid.

### 4. Non-urgent evidence-relevant changes

A discretionary, non-urgent administrative change that could affect a relied-upon Decision 0009 premise should be reviewed before it is made.

Examples include material changes to:

macOS platform behavior; filesystem or APFS configuration; ACL or security semantics; startup-disk or startup-volume-group selection; System/Data relationships; mounts; firmlinks; aliases; backing locations; cleanup configuration; relevant ownership, mode, ACL, or flag state; or the location or identity of a protected binding.

The Human Chair may authorize such a change.

Where an approved change affects an existing qualification, reliance on that affected qualification is suspended until the changed premise has been appropriately re-established.

Ordinary unrelated use of the Mac does not require Decision 0009 review merely because preservation obligations are active.

### 5. Startup, namespace, and mount changes

No discretionary change to a relied-upon startup, namespace, mount, or backing-location premise may silently inherit an earlier Decision 0009 qualification.

This includes relevant changes to:

the default startup installation or volume group; System/Data pairing; a relied-upon mount relationship; material symlink, firmlink, alias, or backing relationships; accepted ancestors; or namespace mappings affecting a protected path.

If such a change is necessary and authorized, the affected qualification must be treated as suspended until the relevant evidence is re-established.

That qualification may not be relied upon again until the changed premise has been re-established under the applicable evidence method.

Unaffected qualifications, evidence, and governance decisions remain valid.

Uncertainty about whether an affected premise still holds must not be converted into an assumed PASS.

a tranche is completed; a review finishes; a gate completes; a later stage begins; an artifact is sealed; or a model/agent completes its assignment.

P, I, the eventual O, Q once created, and any relied-upon parent or ancestor namespace remain subject to the applicable preservation constraints until:
