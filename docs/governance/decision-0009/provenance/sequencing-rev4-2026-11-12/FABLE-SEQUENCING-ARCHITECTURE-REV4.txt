## F. CP1 / CP2 analysis

CP1 and CP2 are requirements, not permissions. Absence of protected-fact reliance satisfies neither and authorizes nothing. Both are defined in the Register for the narrow profile; §D states what governs broader scope in the meantime.

**Classification rule: abstract adoption or concrete-design adoption.** The rule looks at what adopting a provision would do, never at what the provision is called.

- **Abstract adoption** settles *what* must be true. It establishes obligations, properties, limits, evidence requirements, or design questions deliberately left open.
- **Concrete-design adoption** settles *how* an obligation will be met, or lets later work treat a particular answer to "how" as settled.

Apply it provision by provision:

1. **Read for effect.** Set the provision's label aside. Calling something a requirement, an alternative, a symbolic binding, an abstract interface or an outcome constraint describes it; the label exempts nothing.
2. **Ask what adoption would settle.** The provision is concrete design if adopting it would do any of these:
   - choose the particular procedure or component by which something is done or enforced;
   - fix the path that evidence or capabilities travel, or the arrangement of the parts that handle them;
   - fix the representation in which results or evidence are expressed or stored;
   - identify which actual components carry the enforcing responsibilities;
   - distribute capacity among the parts of a particular design, as distinct from stating a limit every design must respect;
   - make the project depend on a particular external component, version or behavior;
   - accept a particular argument that a given design conforms.
3. **Ask what later work could build on.** The provision is also concrete design if, once adopted, later work could properly treat any answer of the kind in step 2 as decided and proceed on it.
4. **Result.** If step 2 or step 3 applies, applicable CP2 closure is required before adoption. If neither applies, the provision is abstract and may be adopted as architecture without closing CP2.
5. **Mixed artifacts.** Classify each provision separately. Abstract provisions may proceed; concrete ones wait for CP2.
6. **Doubt.** Treat an unresolved classification as concrete design until you resolve it.

The rule is meant to capture the substance of the Register's CP2 text, which names concrete methods, routes, capacity allocations, dependencies and design-level conformance arguments. Where my wording and the Register differ, the Register controls.

Two factors inform the rule without deciding it:

- **Open alternatives.** Keeping materially different compliant realizations open supports an abstract classification. It is not required, and listing alternatives does not rescue a provision that settles on one.
- **Backend population.** The number of known compatible backends does not decide classification. A realization that many backends share is still a realization. An obligation that one known candidate satisfies, or none, is still abstract. A small population is a prompt to check the provision under §G.

## G. Backend comparison / recommendation / selection boundary

| State | What it is | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| **Documentary comparison** | Candidates compared against obligations | Needs a specific assignment; carries no selection authority |
| **Feasibility finding** | A determination that a candidate does or does not satisfy an obligation | A finding; not selection |
| **Recommendation or ranking** | Advice to you, where the assignment asks for it | Not selection unless governance says otherwise |
| **Consequential disposition** | Removing a candidate from the authorized process, or making later work depend on one candidate | Requires appropriate authority; analysis alone cannot do it |
| **Backend selection** | Your decision after the required CP1 showing | Human Chair only |

**Selection in effect.** A report can select without using the word. It does so if it commits downstream work to one candidate, stops analysis of the others on its own initiative, or sets a requirement shaped to fit one candidate. Under Option 1, any report that would have that effect must stop and return the question to you.

**Truthful feasibility.** Where the evidence shows a candidate cannot meet an applicable obligation, the analysis says so. A candidate is not reported as feasible in order to keep more than one name on the list. The finding is still a finding; removing the candidate from the process and choosing among the rest remain your acts.

**Requirements come before candidate count.** A sound safety, evidence, continuity, capacity or governance requirement is not relaxed so that more backends pass. The number of surviving candidates yields to the adopted requirements. A report may accurately conclude that one candidate, or none, is feasible at present, and that conclusion is not a backend selection.
