**Premise timing: a policy choice exposed for you (CH-N06).**

- **CR-28 [N · abstract · Human-Chair-adoptable policy choice]. Option B is proposed.** Adopted sources set the minimum at the first dependent effect (SUCC R-38; ABE-A NB-3; ABE P-1R4). They do not require every empirical premise to be established before design closure. Option B follows that minimum, with three premise kinds:

| Kind | Test | Treatment at closure |
|---|---|---|
| **Design-validity** | The item cannot be judged lawful, credible or sufficient without it. This includes any premise that adopted text makes part of that judgment, and any fact needed to classify an IM act | **Established before closure.** Not schedulable |
| **Pre-effect** | Establishable without the governed operation; needed before some dependent effect; not needed to judge the design | **May be scheduled** under CR-29 |
| **Execution-observable** | Obtainable only through the authorized event | **May be carried** under CR-30 |

- **CR-29 [N · abstract]. Scheduling conditions.** A pre-effect premise may be scheduled only if the showing:
  1. classifies it and gives its complete acquisition design;
  2. names the specific authority and predecessor gates its acquisition needs, none of which is on hold or otherwise unavailable;
  3. names its first dependent effect and a discharge point strictly before that effect;
  4. shows that no dependent effect can occur while it is undischarged;
  5. states that if it proves false the item loses closed status.
- **CR-30 [G · abstract]. Carrying conditions.** An execution-observable premise may be carried only if the showing reserves its observation route, defines the admissible observation and its failure or uncertainty classification, shows behavior stays safe and bounded if it is false, and names the stop. No operation may require the fact it is meant to produce (SUCC R-38).
- **CR-31 [G · abstract].** Naming a stop does not turn a pre-effect premise into an execution-observable one. A premise needed before a lawful or safe first effect is never deferred into the governed operation (SUCC R-38; REG PO-21 as pattern).
- **CR-32 [N · abstract].** Scheduling is not authority and acquires nothing. A closed item with undischarged premises is closed subject to them, and its provenance says so.
- **CR-33 [N · alternative, not proposed]. Option A.** You may instead adopt the stricter rule that no pre-effect E premise may be scheduled. Its rationale is simplicity and a stronger fail-closed posture. Its cost is that empirical acquisition must precede design closure, possibly for designs never used. If you choose it, CR-29 is removed and pre-effect premises are treated as design-validity premises. Neither option is source-required.
- **CR-34 [N · abstract]. Never closure evidence:** protected content; unauthorized observation; reviewer acceptance; your adoption; a producer's unsupported account, including a Chair-supplied value; a label, name, status or digest standing alone.

## 11. Dependency and non-circularity rules

- **CR-35 [N · abstract]. Order follows reliance.** No item closes before the items its claims rely on, unless they are determined together as a set.
- **CR-36 [N · abstract]. Grouping justifies nothing.** Placing every member of a dependency cycle in one set does not satisfy GC-5 or GC-7.
- **CR-37 [N · abstract]. Grounding rule.** A claim is grounded only if it has at least one complete line of support in which:
  1. every end point is a grounding base; and
  2. no claim supports itself, directly or through other claims.

  Every claim of every item in a set must be grounded.
- **CR-38 [N · abstract]. Grounding bases.** Only these qualify:
  - adopted governing text;
  - an authoritative static source;
  - lawfully acquired and admitted empirical evidence;
  - a scheduled or carried premise meeting §10, in which case the conclusion stays conditional on it;
  - a claim of an item closed outside the set, whose own support does not pass through the set;
  - what the item itself specifies as its design, as distinct from any assertion that the design conforms.
- **CR-39 [N · abstract]. Item cycles versus claim cycles.** Items may rely on each other, provided the claims can be ordered so that each rests only on grounding bases and on claims earlier in that order. Where two or more claims have no support except one another, none of them is grounded and the set does not close.
- **CR-40 [N · abstract]. Independence of a base.** Each asserted base is traced. If its own validity depends on a claim in the set, it is not a base for that set.
- **CR-41 [N · abstract]. How cycles are tested.**
  - The showing makes the reliance structure of the set ascertainable at the level of claims.
  - It identifies every group of items that rely on one another and gives, for each claim in the group, a line of support meeting CR-37.
  - The verifier independently looks for any claim all of whose support returns to itself, and traces each asserted base under CR-40.
  - A showing that understates reliance is defective (§20).
- **CR-42 [G · abstract].** No completeness, provenance, transfer or accounting conclusion rests solely on the account of the producer under assessment (SUCC R-25). No acquisition establishes its own prerequisite (ABE AB-90).
- **CR-43 [S/G · abstract].** These reliance relations hold by adopted text:

| Relation | Source |
|---|---|
| The verifier-independence arrangement is resolved before a PB or custody design relying on it | ABE §24 UQ-9 |
| The M-A/M-B choice is resolved before a route is committed | ABE §24 UQ-4 |
| An IM act is classified, with authority available, before a PB depending on it is credible | ABE AB-90 |
| Static semantics come before the classification that interprets them | ABE AB-29R4, AB-88 |
| The sensitivity envelope is defined before suitability is shown against it | ABE AB-76R4 |
| Accounting classification is resolved before authorization | ABE §24 UQ-12 |

- **CR-44 [N · abstract].** This candidate names no anchor, method or base for any element.
- **CR-45 [G · abstract]. Dependency tags.** Each premise and dependency carries exactly one SUCC R-39 tag: generic or pre-gate; P0-dependent; B5-dependent; Approval-B-dependent; later operational. Separately, and never in place of the tag, the showing states the evidence class, the premise kind, and the governed event or authority that supplies it. A premise obtainable only through the Approval B event is tagged Approval-B-dependent. An item resting on a later-operational fact cannot close.

## 20. Reopening and original-defect rules

- **CR-96 [N · abstract]. Two grounds.** An item ceases to be usable as closed for future reliance on either ground:

| Ground | When it operates | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Supervening invalidation** | A later event defeats a relied-on proposition | Material change to the item; change or loss of status of an item relied on; a superseded or contradicted source; new lawful evidence against a premise; a scheduled or carried premise failing or its route becoming unavailable; a governance change to a mapped mandatory output |
| **Original defect** | On discovery that a criterion was not satisfied when the determination was made. **No later change is needed** | An unidentified dependency; understated reliance; a false or unsupported premise; evidence that never bore on the present subject as asserted; a hidden loop in the reliance structure; an unmapped applicable requirement; use of protected content or unauthorized observation; a verification lacking eligibility, identity basis or scope |

- **CR-97 [N · abstract].** The examples are not exhaustive. Any material failure of any criterion in §§8–11, as of the determination, is an original defect.
- **CR-98 [N · abstract]. Materiality.** A matter is material if it could change whether a criterion is satisfied for a proposition the closure relies on. A change or error that cannot do so does not reopen the item. It is noted in the provenance, with the reason it is immaterial.
- **CR-99 [G · abstract].** Doubt about materiality is treated as material (HP H13, generalized). While the doubt stands, future reliance is suspended.
- **CR-100 [N · abstract]. Prospective effect.** From recognition of either ground:
  - no new commitment, incorporation or dependent closure may rest on the item;
  - a commitment already made on it supports no new reliance;
  - late discovery is no reason to continue relying on it.
- **CR-101 [N · abstract]. Propagation.** Every item with a claim resting on an affected claim is suspended. Suspension lifts only when the relied-on item is closed again and independent verification confirms the relied-on claims are materially unchanged. Otherwise the dependent needs its own renewed closure. Package evaluations including an affected item lapse to the extent affected.
- **CR-102 [N · abstract]. History.** Records of the original showing, verification and determination are preserved unaltered, with the later ground recorded beside them. Which ground applied is itself recorded. Events that lawfully occurred under the authority then understood are not recharacterized by this arrangement.
- **CR-103 [G · abstract].** After an authorized event, later reliance on its outputs follows the receiving domain's rules (ABE AB-86(6) in principle).
- **CR-104 [X].** This candidate does not decide whether an authorization issued on a defective closure was valid. That question is yours.
- **CR-105 [N · abstract]. Renewed closure.** It needs a showing revised to the extent affected, verification of the whole revised showing (D10 clause 4), and a fresh determination.
- **CR-106 [N · abstract].** Loss of closed status is a status consequence. It authorizes no inspection, acquisition, cleanup or other act.
