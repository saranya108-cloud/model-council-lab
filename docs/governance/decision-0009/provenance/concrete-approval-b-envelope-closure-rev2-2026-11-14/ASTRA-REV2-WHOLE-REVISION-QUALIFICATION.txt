## F. Circularity / grounding assessment

**Sound.**

CR-37 requires a complete, non-self-supporting justification for every claim. CR-40 tests whether an apparent base depends back on the set.

| Structure | Outcome |
|---|---|
| A → B → A, with no independent support | Cannot close |
| A → B → C → A, with no independent support | Cannot close |
| Mutual item relationships with independently grounded claims | May close |
| “External” anchor whose validity depends on the set | Rejected as a grounding base |
| One circular line and one complete sound line | Sound line may establish the claim; circular line contributes nothing |

CR-38 permits the item’s description to establish **what design is proposed**, not that the design conforms. Scheduled/carried premises support only expressly conditional conclusions.

## G. Original-defect / reopening assessment

**Sound.**

CR-96 distinguishes:

- a valid closure subsequently defeated; and
- a closure whose criteria were never satisfied.

The original-defect category covers omitted dependencies, understated reliance, unsupported premises, hidden circularity, omitted requirements, verifier defects and prohibited inputs.

CR-98–99 correctly separate demonstrably harmless editorial changes from material changes and unresolved doubt. CR-100 stops prospective reliance even through an existing commitment. CR-101 propagates the consequence through affected claims and packages.

CR-102 preserves historical records. CR-104 reserves the validity of an authorization issued on defective closure to the Human Chair. A dependent item with its **own** original defect cannot use CR-101’s suspension-lifting provision to avoid CR-105 renewed closure.
