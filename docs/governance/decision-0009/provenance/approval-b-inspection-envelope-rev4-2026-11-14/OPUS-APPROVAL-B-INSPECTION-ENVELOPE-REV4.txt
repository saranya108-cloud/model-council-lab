## 6. Acquisition, visibility and recording boundary

### 6.1 Actors and scopes

**AB-13R [N].**

1. **Acquisition component (AC).** A bounded component that acquires the complete bytes of each enumerated object once and computes Tier A. Whole-file acquisition is needed for M-1. It does not mean every actor receives plaintext.
2. **Plaintext recipient (PR).** Any person, model, service, tool or intermediary that receives protected plaintext, whole or partial, where a MOS output or authorized optional item needs one.
3. **Non-plaintext recipient.** An actor that receives only Tier A values, records without verbatim protected content, or reports.

The four scopes:

- **AAS (authorized acquisition scope):** the complete bytes of the enumerated objects, acquired by the AC. It also covers IM, but only where an IM act is lawfully classified and authorized under §9.3.
- **EVS (executor visibility scope):** set per actor by the authorization. AC: the whole bytes. Each PR: what its authorized exposure mechanism delivers. Non-plaintext recipients: no plaintext. **No actor has EVS by default.**
- **RS (recording scope):** content that answers a MOS output or authorized optional item; IC-0; lawful IM; and mandatory process records (per-object scope note, stop records, incident records, completeness notes, manifest).
- **EIC (excluded and incident content):** content that must not be retained beyond governed instances, transcribed, summarized, characterized or relied on.

**AB-14R [N]. Resolution of UQ-1.**

- Acquisition: whole object, by the AC.
- Visibility: per actor, never universal.
- Recording: limited to MOS and authorized items.
- Reliance: limited to later admission.

Screening is optional. If used, it is a governed actor and must be suitable for the full content (AB-76R4). Its output is a governed transformation (AB-31). [D: Approval B authorization]

### 6.3 Secrets and sensitive content

**AB-15R [N].**

1. **Before exposure.** Every plaintext route is suitable under AB-76R4. No pre-read guarantee that secrets are absent is required.
2. **On discovery on a suitable route:**
   - never transcribe, quote, summarize, use or test the secret;
   - make a locator-only incident record;
   - mark the holding instances secret-bearing;
   - exclude the secret's range from excerpts.

   An output or item that can be answered only from that range becomes `UNRESOLVED`. If the secret affects an item's meaning, omitting the bytes does not make the item answerable.

   **A MOS output that becomes unresolved this way is an execution-time failure** (AB-86 item 6). It does not invalidate the authorization.
3. **Continuation** of other outputs requires affirmatively established independence (AB-44).
4. **Reporting** gives class and location, never content. Any response (rotation, revocation, disposal) needs separate authority.
5. **Unsuitable recipient.** A secret that reaches an unsuitable recipient is an out-of-route exposure incident (AB-16R; S-4; T1). Handling it afterwards does not cure the disclosure.

Making secret discovery a T1 stop remains an optional choice at authorization (NB-5).

### 6.4 Exposure outside the AAS or an EVS

**AB-16R [N].**

1. **Prevention.** Each route's pre-exposure design (AB-75) shows it is bounded: no listing, previews, automatic loading of neighbors, indexing, writable descriptors (PLAN §J), or undeclared production.
2. **If exposure occurs anyway:**
   - stop within existing authority (S-4, T1);
   - preserve the uncertainty;
   - record only what is lawful (class, medium, time, actor; no content);
   - claim no inventory closure and no safe retention unless established;
   - inspect no unknown media unless separately authorized;
   - clean or delete nothing without separate authority;
   - report to the Chair.
3. Stopping is not exposure control. Prevention is.

## 9. Provenance and source identity

### 9.1 Tier A: acquired-byte identity

**AB-25R [N].** Tier A records the length, digest and snapshot identity of the bytes the AC acquired. It is recomputed at each declared custody checkpoint.

- **Establishes:** byte equality at each checked point, under stated integrity assumptions (algorithm, computing component, protection of the comparison record).
- **Does not establish:** an uninterrupted history without change, attribution, coherence, no substitution, link or type, or stability (CON §4; H15; R-26; R-27).

### 9.2 Tier B (original-acquisition provenance) and Tier C (later interval)

**AB-26 [N; from CON §4 items 1, 2 and 8; CON §7; H15; R-26; R-27].** A relationship is `ESTABLISHED` only through a proof basis (PB) that:

1. is lawfully classifiable and authorized (§9.3; R-32);
2. is independently reviewable;
3. does not rest solely on a producer's account (this includes a Chair-supplied digest);
4. uses no B5 observation and no empirical acquisition lacking its required authority;
5. was documented before authorization.

Independence does not require an endless chain of witnesses. Static assurance, separately protected anchors and original observations may each contribute parts of a proof (AST-R3 §H). No class is sufficient merely because it is called independent.

### 9.3 Identity-metadata authority (resolves R3-B2)

**AB-80R4 [N]. No blanket rule.** Existing governance establishes **no blanket rule that all identity metadata is inside Approval B, and no blanket rule that all identity metadata is outside it**. PLAN §§J and N describe a bounded read-only inspection. They neither expressly authorize all identity metadata nor expressly exclude every metadata operation incidental to identifying and reading the authorized object. REV4 §H allows envelope architecture to define permitted kinds of observation.

Classification therefore depends on the exact fact, method, semantics, substantive effect and governing authority, and is decided **method by method and claim by claim**.

**Finding about the reviewed record:** no IM method has been classified or qualified for Approval B, and none is authorized here. This describes the record. It does not claim the category is empty.

**AB-29R4 [N]. Classification.** Every proposed IM or source-identification act is classified by its effect before it can be part of an executable envelope. The test:

- what exact fact the act acquires;
- what static semantics its interpretation needs;
- what empirical applicability premises it needs;
- whether it touches anything beyond the authorized object (other objects, parents, namespace state);
- whether it characterizes or excludes agents;
- what exact governing text reaches it.

Exactly one class applies:

1. **Within Approval B's bounded protected inspection.** Permissible only where an exact, source-based analysis of the proposed act, reviewed independently, shows the act falls within the existing inspection authority (PLAN §§J, N, read with REV4 §H). Necessary conditions, none sufficient alone:
   - it concerns only the authorized object reached by its authorized path;
   - it is incidental to identifying or reading that object;
   - it uses no writable descriptors;
   - it resolves no other object, parent or namespace state;
   - it acquires no empirical platform premise;
   - it characterizes no agent;
   - its result serves only the stated source-identification relationship.

   **No method qualifies merely because** it concerns one of the three files, is read-only, is metadata, is convenient, or is not B5.
2. **A separate empirical, platform or filesystem observation.** Requires specific prior authority and applicable predecessors (R-32; CON §4 last paragraph; REG §3, class E). Where it acquires platform premises, it is host observation and must respect the unopened host-observation envelope (R-32).
3. **B5-governed.** Only where the method or its reliance requires characterizing or excluding autonomous agents, or another B5-governed proposition (REG §5; R-23).
4. **Currently prohibited or unavailable**, where the required authority or prerequisite is unavailable.

**Doubt goes to the more restrictive class.** A class 1 determination for a concrete method is a concrete commitment, governed by AB-81 (§14.5). Naming an IM act in an authorization does not make it lawful. "Not B5" never implies class 1. No universal B5 prerequisite applies to metadata.

**AB-88 [S/N]. Static semantics versus empirical applicability.**

- **[S]** REG §3: **S, static assurance**, covers design and feasibility "from exact implementation/configuration, authoritative semantics, capability and route analysis…". It is "insufficient for actual deployment or execution claims." **E, empirical premise evidence**, covers "actual deployment, configuration, platform, agent population, runtime properties, or protection boundaries". Its "acquisition requires specific authority and all applicable predecessor gates." SUCC §E.0 keeps the S, E and X terms.
- **[N] Static or documentary semantic assurance** (reading authoritative documentation, reasoning about documented API or filesystem semantics, establishing what a method *would* mean if its applicability premises hold) is **not host observation** merely because its subject is platform or filesystem semantics. During design it needs only the specific assignment under which it is done. During an event it is subject to AB-18R4 item 12.
- **[N] Empirical applicability evidence** (that the actual environment shows a behavior, or that a particular implementation, version or configuration is present) is class E. It needs the authority the proposition requires, which may be platform or host authority (R-32; CON §4).
- **[N]** The two are not collapsed. Documentation or an API or provider name does not establish actual applicability. Lacking empirical applicability does not invalidate static analysis. It blocks only reliance that needs the applicability.

**AB-89 [N]. IM classification template.** This is a template, not permission. It is deliberately left unpopulated.

| Proposed IM act | Exact fact acquired | Static semantics needed (S) | Empirical applicability premise (E) | Authority classification (AB-29R4 class 1–4, with source basis) | PB relationship supported (AR-1/2/3, C-1/2) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *[method, by name and scope]* | *[the fact, and only that]* | *[documented semantics relied on, with sources]* | *[actual-environment premise, with acquisition route and authority, or "none needed"]* | *[class and basis; doubt → restrictive]* | *[relationship and argument; see AB-90]* | *[unclassified / classified / qualified / unavailable]* |

Astra's guidance (AST-R3 §E), kept unpopulated:

- object type: no blanket determination;
- link status: unresolved in the abstract, and resolving other objects, parents or namespace state may cross the boundary;
- same-handle change indicator: needs analysis of the observation, its semantics and any applicability premises;
- other metadata: no default either way;
- agent characterization: class 3 where that reliance is present.

**AB-90 [N]. Authority and sufficiency are separate.** A lawful classification, including class 1, does **not** establish that a method is a sufficient PB. Sufficiency is determined separately:

- what relationship the method purports to prove;
- what static semantics it relies on;
- what applicability premises it needs;
- whether those premises are established lawfully;
- whether it is independently reviewable and non-circular.

A PB depending on an IM act counts as credible under AB-28R4 only if that act is lawfully classifiable with its authority available, **and** it meets this sufficiency test. No acquisition may establish its own prerequisite.
