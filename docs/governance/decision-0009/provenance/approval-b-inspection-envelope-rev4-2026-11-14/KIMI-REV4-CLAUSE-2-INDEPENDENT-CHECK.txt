E. P1–P5 findings
P1 — No blanket rule placing every identity-metadata observation inside Approval B
SUPPORTED
PLAN §J authorizes consideration of only a future single bounded read-only inspection of three enumerated objects, with no writable descriptors. It does not say that every observation characterized as identity metadata falls within that inspection.
More importantly, SUCC R-32 requires specific prior authority for each empirical or execution observation and states that acquiring platform premises is host observation subject to the unopened host-observation envelope. SUCC §L separately places actual runtime behavior, process state, deployed configuration, installed versions, and actual runtime mismatch outside the document read.
Therefore, the “identity metadata” label cannot override those method- and proposition-specific limits.
P2 — No blanket rule placing every identity-metadata observation outside Approval B
SUPPORTED
PLAN §§J and N do not expressly exclude every metadata operation associated with identifying or reading an authorized object. Sequencing Revision 4 §H expressly permits a future inspection-envelope architecture to define the kinds of observation that would be allowed.
SUCC §L does not create the asserted categorical exclusion: it expressly describes itself as a requirements dependency map, not an inspection envelope or the PLAN §J output list. Omission of a metadata method from that map therefore cannot establish a blanket prohibition.
This is only a no-blanket-exclusion finding. It does not affirmatively place any concrete method inside Approval B.
P3 — Method-specific and effect-based classification is required
SUPPORTED
Evidence Contract §2 requires each claim to identify its operation or mechanism, material premises, intended reliance, observations, dependencies, and limits. Material mechanism or premise changes are evaluated separately.
Sequencing Revision 4 §§D–F likewise requires classification by substantive reliance and effect rather than terminology. SUCC R-38 requires every material premise to be classified, while R-32 separately governs acquisition authority.
Consequently, the exact fact acquired, method, semantics, empirical premises, objects touched, agent reliance, substantive effect, and governing text must be assessed claim by claim.
P4 — “Not B5” does not imply “authorized by Approval B”
SUPPORTED
B5 governs particular agent-characterization and exclusion propositions; Register §5 and SUCC R-23 do not make it the only applicable authority boundary.
A method that requires no B5-governed fact must still satisfy, as applicable:
- PLAN §§J and N’s object and inspection limits;
- SUCC R-32’s specific acquisition-authority requirement;
- platform/host-observation restrictions;
- proposition-specific evidence and predecessor requirements.
Absence of a B5 dependency therefore supplies no affirmative Approval B authority.
P5 — Calling something “metadata” establishes neither permission nor prohibition
SUPPORTED
SUCC §K invariant 6 states that ambient platform metadata is neither presumed governed nor presumed exempt and requires a justified boundary determination. Although that invariant concerns accounting rather than Approval B permission, it directly confirms that the label “metadata” does not settle classification.
SUCC R-27 separately provides that names, paths, inodes, digests, and similar identifiers do not independently establish governed identity. R-32 governs acquisition authority independently of the identifier’s label. SUCC §L also distinguishes declared information from actual deployed or runtime facts.
Permission and prohibition therefore follow from the actual operation and governing authority, not the word “metadata.”
F. S1–S5 findings
S1 — Static semantic assurance can be performed without itself constituting host/platform observation
SUPPORTED WITH QUALIFICATION
Register §3 classifies design and feasibility analysis from authoritative semantics, exact implementation/configuration, capability and route analysis as S, distinct from E evidence about actual deployment and platform properties. Sequencing Revision 4 §H permits documentary envelope architecture under a bounded assignment without acquiring platform premises.
The qualification is essential:
- the analysis must remain genuinely documentary or semantic;
- it requires the applicable specific assignment;
- it must use no protected facts or unauthorized empirical acquisition;
- documentation asserting actual deployed state is not converted into static semantics merely by being read.
SUCC R-32 continues to govern any acquisition of actual platform premises.
S2 — Actual applicability is a separate empirical proposition
SUPPORTED
Register §3 distinguishes S from actual deployment, configuration, platform, runtime, and protection-boundary evidence. PO-01 expressly assigns S to native semantics and E to deployment-dependent applicability. PO-21 similarly distinguishes premise-inventory and acquisition-feasibility analysis from actual-state observations.
SUCC §L reinforces the distinction: declared configuration is not evidence of what is installed, loaded, or executing.
S3 — Required empirical applicability evidence must be obtained under its governing authority
SUPPORTED
Register §3 states that E-class acquisition requires specific authority and all applicable predecessor gates. SUCC R-32 repeats that requirement and adds the host-observation restriction for platform premises. Evidence Contract §4 likewise requires specific observation authority for platform-premise acquisition.
The applicable authority remains proposition-specific; this finding does not select Approval B, B5, or another route for any concrete method.
S4 — Static semantics alone do not establish actual applicability
SUPPORTED
Register §3 expressly states that static assurance alone is insufficient for actual deployment or execution claims. PO-21 rejects the substitution of generic documentation for deployed correspondence. SUCC §L states that a document read establishes declarations, not deployed or executing reality.
S5 — Missing empirical applicability evidence limits reliance without invalidating static analysis
SUPPORTED
Register §3 preserves S as valid evidence for design and feasibility while denying its sufficiency for actual deployment or execution claims. PO-21 rejects documentation as a substitute for deployed correspondence; it does not reject the underlying semantic analysis.
Register CP1 and §8 require static questions answerable at the current stage to be resolved while recording remaining gated dependencies. Thus missing E evidence blocks the conclusions that need applicability. If that missing evidence prevents a credible feasibility showing or is itself a present-stage prerequisite, the affected showing remains unresolved or gated—but the narrower static analysis is not thereby rendered invalid.
