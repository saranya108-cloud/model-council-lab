3. Architectural review
3.1 Claim profile, oracle, and event schedule
The two-case profile is adequately bounded. It does not claim general exclusive-creation qualification and expressly excludes link variants, races, allocation, comprehensive zero coverage, crash survival, resource limits, operational custody, and universal side-effect freedom. That is a permissible narrowing under the adopted Level-2 contract, not an unacknowledged omission of negative-case coverage.
The E5 refusal oracle must be independently sourced, revision-bound, mapped to native results, and fixed before the first governed effect. Observed outcomes cannot retrospectively select or alter it.
The seven-event schedule is closed:
- E1–E5 are the complete ordinary acquisition sequence.
- E6/E7 are fixed, predeclared terminal capability-closure actions, not unspecified cleanup.
- E5 always ends ordinary dispatch after its single attempt, including on timeout or interruption.
- No slot can be repeated, replaced, or supplemented by an eighth subject event.
- Failure or uncertainty in E1–E4 prevents dependent dispatch.
- X-L is an evidence exception record, not another subject operation.
Dispatch eligibility is properly separated from predicates that can only be evaluated over the later dispatch interval. In particular, Draft 4 does not pretend that a pre-dispatch check alone proves E2 absence or E3→E5 continuity through the subsequent dispatch boundary.
3.2 In-flight execution and no-overlap guarantees
Draft 4’s separation of execution state from reporting disposition is sound.
Both IN_FLIGHT and EXECUTOR_LOST — EFFECT_INTERVAL_UNRESOLVED prevent every later subject operation, including E6/E7, whenever the earlier operation or its delegated effects may continue. Definite and merely possible dispatch both invoke this prohibition.
The architecture correctly provides that:
- Timeout, abandonment, cancellation intent, reporting interruption, and a stop decision are not completion.
- Component loss is not effect cessation.
- A hung component is not automatically a lost component.
- An asynchronous submission acknowledgement is not a terminal operation return.
- No cancellation operation or completion probe is silently introduced.
- Parent or target closure cannot be inferred from missing output.
- A frozen “unavailable” report does not assert physical quiescence.
A synchronous backend can conform without artificial asynchronous machinery, provided its normal terminal return has the required effect-interval completion semantics. If a synchronous call hangs or its completion meaning cannot be established, later dispatch remains blocked and reporting may be incomplete.
I found no path that permits a later subject event or premature parent/subject closure while an earlier operation may still act.
3.3 Dispatch evidence and complete mediation
The five dispatch/evidence classifications do not replace execution state. Positive non-dispatch proof must cover the interval beginning when the slot first became reachable or eligible; missing prepared, issued, completion, or journal records is insufficient.
The mediation scope includes the relevant bypass surfaces:
- Direct, inherited, and aliased references.
- Ambient namespace access.
- Helpers, callbacks, controllers, subprocesses, and privileged intermediaries.
- Framework mechanisms.
- Independent indexing, backup, synchronization, cleanup, and comparable system agents.
G’s own journal cannot establish the absence of bypass. The required independent execution-path, configuration, restriction, and interval evidence prevents the design from becoming self-certifying. System-agent exclusions remain subject to separate observation authority and do not reopen B5.
3.4 Late E1/E3/E5 returns and residual capabilities
The general confinement rule safely handles late returns from E1, E3, and E5.
A late return cannot:
- Establish a new P or L3.
- Become an E6/E7 target.
- Reopen ordinary dispatch or a frozen slot.
- Create an eighth subject event.
- Authorize inspection, retry, cancellation, cleanup, closure, or continuation.
- Rewrite an earlier frozen reporting disposition.
- Manufacture a new source record outside the declared routes.
- Establish an otherwise unavailable positive qualification.
“Confined, not proven closed” is the correct treatment. It distinguishes technical non-invocability from unproven capability destruction or physical cessation. No destructor, process exit, passive expiration, or teardown is treated as an implicit cleanup mechanism.
E6/E7 must neither invoke nor invalidate confined capabilities through aliasing, ownership, lifetime, or parent dependencies. If that independence is unavailable, the affected terminal action becomes unavailable rather than becoming a substitute cleanup operation.
3.5 X-L boundedness
X-L is architecturally bounded under the stated conformance obligations:
- It occupies one of the four already reserved attempt-level exception slots.
- It does not replace or mutate a sealed source record.
- It preserves the original event association, prior disposition, arrival interval, result, confinement state, and limitations.
- It is accounted through M-R/J-R before final reconciliation.
- G has only the declared bounded route; receiver/store admission remains constrained.
- It requires no unassigned governed object or additional transport intermediary.
- It closes before the final creation/accounting frontier.
Serial dispatch and freezing on unresolved execution leave at most one original subject operation unresolved at the stop. Thus the normal X-L case is bounded to one original late return, not an open-ended callback log. Duplicate or multiplied arrivals are a completeness failure, not permission to allocate more records or select a favorable version.
A backend needing an additional spool, callback log, transport intermediate, or receiver artifact fails X-L route fit. It cannot silently enlarge the architecture.
Returns outside the authorized capture window remain unavailable to the experiment. Their prior unresolved execution state remains visible, and their capabilities remain confined. Post-window arrival cannot repair closure or create an undeclared finalization artifact.
3.6 Source sealing and finite finalization
The source-sealing order is correct:
1. Ordinary dispatch ends.
2. E6/E7 receive attempted, withheld, or unavailable treatment under their predicates.
3. All E1–E7 reporting dispositions freeze while execution state remains distinct.
4. Source records settle as complete, partial, missing, or unavailable.
5. L-S and the source manifest record the resulting state.
6. Source writing closes and the designated source set seals.
An unresolved operation can therefore be reported honestly without waiting indefinitely or claiming physical quiescence. Later observations use the declared pre-seal source route or post-seal X-L route according to the cutoff; they do not mutate the sealed ten-source transfer set.
Finalization is finite:
- X-L closes within its fixed window.
- R-I/R-C reconcile production and charges.
- E, C, T0, and T1 are established and charged before the creation frontier.
- The frontier then closes to every new governed artifact.
- E/T0, C, and T1 are produced or recorded once in their pre-established objects.
- Processing ends after T1 or its unavailability.
The pre-establishment rule resolves the apparent frontier/production ordering issue: later writes do not create new governed artifacts after the frontier.
There is no T2, recursive self-authentication, post-closure repair, or unbounded wait. C does not certify its own future write or T1; T1 does not certify its own persistence or delivery. Missing T1 means incomplete or unproven closure.
3.7 Enforcement model and protection domains
The effective-capability definition is objectively assessable. It covers callable interfaces, ambient rights, inherited references, mutable configuration, dynamic code, helpers, credentials, debug facilities, and escape paths. A policy statement alone cannot remove a technically reachable operation.
The closed enforcing set—G, store-enforcement mechanisms, M-S, M-T, and M-R—is coherent because:
- Exact implementation/configuration and deployment identities must be fixed.
- Permitted entry points, operations, targets, parameters, phases, and bounds must be enumerated.
- Initialization, return, timeout, callback, failure, and teardown paths are included.
- Raw primitives, code loading, debugging, credentials, helpers, and mutable dependencies require escape analysis.
- Reviewed behavioral enforcement is unacceptable where callers can bypass it or obtain raw capabilities.
G may hold a broader primitive only if its assured implementation exposes and executes the bounded event paths and no other component can acquire or command the unrestricted primitive.
M-* may enforce their own bounded transitions without their ledgers becoming independent completeness evidence. Expanding the enforcing set is correctly classified as an architectural revision.
The protection-domain union rule prevents fictitious documentation-only separation. Shared process, address space, credential, runtime, memory, mutable configuration, or raw-access helpers must be evaluated for shared effective capability. Reliance on internal isolation requires implementation, enforcement, deployment, and bypass evidence.
3.8 Evaluated-producer independence
Draft 4 identifies independent bases for all five material evidence questions:
- G/source observations.
- Source-production completeness.
- Transfer completeness.
- Receiver-production completeness.
- Accounting and finalization.
No completeness claim is permitted to rest solely on the evaluated producer’s account. External store constraints, fixed schedules, route analysis, original emission/receipt correspondence, protected bindings, independent configuration evidence, and cross-scope reconciliation supply the required separate bases.
Co-located accountants are not independent witnesses merely because they have different component names. Shared-domain cases remain subject to the capability-union analysis.
3.9 Continuity and evidence handoff
E2→E3: The same parent binding and fixed leaf designation must be covered through E3’s dispatch boundary, including intervening creation, replacement, rebinding, and namespace changes. An unproven interval prevents reliance on the absence premise without erasing E3’s original result.
E3→E5: E4 and subsequent evaluation cover origin, parent binding, same-object designation, regular-file state, substitution and identifier-reuse exclusion, continuing reference/leaf relationships, namespace continuity, and intervening effects through E5 dispatch. This is backend-neutral without reducing identity to a universal POSIX attribute or digest.
Routes A and B: Both are sufficient as architectural route classes:
- Route A requires uninterrupted origin/admission and store-held binding.
- Route B requires protected provenance covering store/container, attempt/slot, origin/version, writer history, seal state, extent, integrity, and substitution exclusions.
- Route B anchors cannot be modified by the artifact writer, reader, sender, receiver, or another party whose account they validate, including through shared domains.
- Digest equality and unverified pathname reopening satisfy neither route.
- Concrete reopening/live-transfer methods remain separately reviewed and adopted as required.
3.10 Production scopes, transfer, retention, and anti-selection
Source admission occurs before source reliance. The eight-input allowance is global, not eight per accounting domain. Cross-domain counting once requires sameness/continuity proof; distinct copies receive distinct charges.
Each source artifact has at most one forward transfer. Declaration, start, emission, receipt, validation, acceptance, and retention remain distinct. Partial transfer is a failure state and grants no retransmission, fallback store, replacement representation, or recapture.
The architecture fails closed against the requested selection and recovery attacks:
- Favorable selection, stale/replayed evidence, wrong-attempt evidence, regenerated records, reordered evidence, and conflicts remain visible and cannot be replaced by a favorable candidate.
- Missing output is not proof of non-dispatch or non-creation.
- Unexpected E5 success is adverse where the occupied-target premise is established; otherwise its premise uncertainty remains explicit.
- Retransmission, resumed attempts, hidden cleanup, first-success stopping, post-hoc recovery, and later-review reconstruction are unauthorized.
- Late returns cannot rewrite earlier dispositions or repair missing positive evidence.
- Retention must be fixed before execution and survives through required review and later proposition-level evaluation until separate release authority.
BP1–BP3 accurately consolidate these obligations without converting their backend evidence prerequisites into already-satisfied facts.
