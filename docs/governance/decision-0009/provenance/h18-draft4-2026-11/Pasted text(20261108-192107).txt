## 7. State-machine / continuity / late-return assessment

I challenged the interactions among `IN_FLIGHT`, `EXECUTOR_LOST — EFFECT_INTERVAL_UNRESOLVED`, `Q`, `U(k)`, possible vs. definite dispatch, post-loss evidence, `E6`/`E7`, frozen dispositions, source sealing, and `X-L`:

1. **No O1 overlap hazard:** Under §2 and `PO-03`, every slot `E1`–`E7` requires `Q` (`No earlier subject operation remains in flight or has an unresolved continuing-effect interval`) and `U(k)`. Whenever dispatch crosses or *may* have crossed the governed boundary, the slot enters `IN_FLIGHT` and `Q` becomes `false`. While `Q` is `false`, neither `E2`–`E5` nor `E6`/`E7` can dispatch.
2. **Timeout and component loss are not completion:** `PO-03` explicitly mandates that timeout, abandonment, cancellation intent, interruption, and stopping do not establish completion. Component disappearance does not prove cessation; if delegated, kernel-mediated, or other effects may continue, the state transitions to `EXECUTOR_LOST — EFFECT_INTERVAL_UNRESOLVED` and `Q` stays `false`. Hung/blocked components are kept distinct from lost components.
3. **Post-loss evidence cannot improperly restore `Q`:** Under `PO-03` (as repaired by `CV1`), `PO-09`, `PO-13`, `PO-14`, and §7, if an executing component, `G`, or another relevant subject, writer, child, framework component, evaluator, runner, or the OS is lost, post-loss evidence can resolve `EXECUTOR_LOST` or possible dispatch **only if** that exact post-loss observation was included in the original authorized sequence and has an applicable adopted continuity method under [Level-2 Evidence Contract §6](file:///Users/aclab/aclab/model-council-lab/docs/governance/decision-0009/DECISION-0009-V2-2B-LEVEL-2-EVIDENCE-CONTRACT.md#L217-L218). Unplanned process reap/exit status, non-dispatch arguments, or diagnostic observations cannot restore `Q`.
4. **Possible-dispatch resolution vs. definite dispatch and `U(k)`:** Under `PO-03` and `PO-09`, if a slot *only possibly dispatched*, positive interval-wide evidence from earliest technical reachability (meeting the post-loss rules if loss occurred) may resolve that dispatch never crossed, restoring `Q` for a *later* bounded terminal action (`E6`/`E7`). However:
   * It **cannot** rewrite a definite dispatch as non-dispatch.
   * It **cannot** reopen or authorize redispatch of slot `k`, because `U(k)` has three independent negative clauses (`has not been attempted, has not become dispatch-uncertain, and has not received a final unavailable/withheld disposition`), and `PO-03` explicitly states that `U(k)` remains `false` once the slot became dispatch-uncertain.
5. **Source-sealing order and late returns (`PO-12`, `PO-13`):**
   * Before dispositions freeze and within the source-finalization bound, an original return can establish completion for `Q` only if `PO-03` (including the Contract §6 post-loss requirement where applicable) is satisfied.
   * Once `E1`–`E7` reporting dispositions are frozen (`PO-12`), the pre-seal source-write route is fenced *before* `L-S` and the source manifest are captured and sealed.
   * A late return arriving after its admission window closes or after dispositions freeze enters a non-invocable compartment (`"confined, not proven closed"`), cannot establish `P` or `L3`, cannot become an `E6`/`E7` target, cannot reopen a frozen slot or alter a reporting disposition, and after source sealing can only be recorded once in the pre-assigned receiver-side `X-L` exception slot within the fixed pre-frontier capture window.
   * `E6`/`E7` must be proven independent of any confined capability (no shared aliasing, ownership, lifetime, or parent invalidation); if independence is unproven, `E6`/`E7` are unavailable (`PO-13`).
6. **Continuity and outcome taxonomy (`PO-07`, `PO-08`, `PO-14`):**
   * `CP4` dispatch eligibility is strictly separated from `CP6` through-dispatch continuity.
   * If continuity through dispatch is **unproven**, the original observation is preserved and the intended proposition is classified as `uncertain`.
   * If continuity through dispatch is **contradicted**, the original observation and contradiction are preserved, the intended proposition is classified as `uncertain` for that attempt, and the mandatory continuity-conflict stop is applied.
   * Missing original observations are classified as `unobserved/unavailable`.
   * Directly adverse observations (such as an unexpected `E5` success) remain visible on their own terms with their premise state, and a failed premise never generates a bogus pass or an unrelated adverse finding.

## 8. Enforcement / provenance / transfer assessment

1. **Enforcing set and capability unions (`PO-04`, `PO-05`):** The register faithfully enforces H18 Draft 4 §8. Ordinary components (`dispatch controller`, `sender`, `receiver/validator`, `evaluator`, helpers) must be externally constrained. Shared protection domains are evaluated as the union of reachable capabilities. The closed enforcing set (`G`, store enforcement, `M-S`, `M-T`, `M-R`) requires exact implementation/configuration identity, entry-point confinement, and escape analysis.
2. **Route A and Route B (`PO-10`, `PO-11`):** `Route A` requires continuous store-held binding from admission through write, seal, and read. Where `Route A` is unproven, `Route B` connects the artifact via a bounded method to a pre-established independently protected provenance anchor. `PO-11` accurately reflects H18 Draft 4 §9.3: the anchor cannot be modified through effective capabilities available to the writer, reader, sender, receiver, or any component whose account it validates (surviving `PO-04` capability-union analysis), and store enforcement may establish/preserve that protection only through a fixed, assured interface without overrides.
3. **Transfer integrity and non-circularity (`PO-15`, `PO-16`, `PO-17`, `PO-20`):**
   * Each of the 10 designated sealed source artifacts (7 event records, 2 acquisition journal/`L-S` objects, 1 source manifest) has at most one forward transfer.

   **No.** Prior over-strengthenings were removed or aligned with Draft 4: no universal durable pre-effect journal is required (`PO-09`), no universal identifier or link-count-one rule is added (`PO-08`), `Route B` explicitly permits store enforcement to maintain anchor protection through its fixed, assured interface per Draft 4 §9.3 (`PO-11`), and single-execution continuity uncertainty is not treated as design incompatibility (`PO-07`).
4. **Can post-loss evidence improperly restore Q?**  
   **No.** Under `PO-03`, `PO-09`, `PO-13`, `PO-14`, and §7, post-loss evidence can resolve `EXECUTOR_LOST` or possible dispatch only if that exact post-loss observation was in the original authorized sequence and has an applicable adopted method under Contract §6. Unplanned reap/exit status, non-dispatch arguments, and diagnostics cannot restore `Q`.
5. **Can E6/E7 execute while an earlier effect may still continue?**  
   **No.** `E6` and `E7` both require `Q` (§2, `PO-03`), which is `false` whenever any earlier subject slot is in flight, only possibly dispatched (until positively resolved across the full reachability interval), or in `EXECUTOR_LOST — EFFECT_INTERVAL_UNRESOLVED`.
6. **Can a late return reopen a frozen slot or change a disposition?**  
   **No.** Under `PO-03`, `PO-12`, and `PO-13`, once slot dispositions freeze, a late return cannot reopen any slot, change a frozen reporting disposition, establish `P` or `L3`, or supply a new `E6`/`E7` target.
7. **Can source sealing become stale?**  
   **No.** `PO-12` requires fencing the pre-seal source-write route *before* capturing the final `L-S` and source-manifest state (satisfying Kimi's retained caution #1). Any return arriving after the fence remains confined until the receiver-side `X-L` route records it without mutating the sealed 10-source transfer set.
8. **Can provenance or completeness become self-certifying?**  
   **No.** `PO-02`, `PO-05`, `PO-09`, `PO-11`, `PO-15`, `PO-16`, and `PO-17` reject reliance on `G`'s journal alone, an accountant's membership in the enforcing set alone, unprotected hashes/labels, sender-only/regenerated logs, or `C`/`T1` self-certification.
9. **Can a 65th support object appear implicitly?**  
   **No.** `PO-19` fixes all 64 governed-support slots across 12 roles with zero object-count headroom and explicitly requires rejection (and architectural revision) if a 65th governed-support object is needed.
10. **Can framing or transport intermediates escape accounting?**  
    **No.** `PO-18` charges framing within the 4,096-byte event record (`512` bytes), and `PO-19` mandates that the 10 byte-exact receiver copies (`53,248` bytes) have no free framing allowance—any framing, metadata, serialization, or `X-L` buffering must fit already-assigned slots/bytes (such as the 20 transport intermediates at `1,310,720` bytes) or the backend is rejected.
11. **Are design-level sufficiency and execution-level reconciliation kept separate?**  
    **Yes.** `PO-23` explicitly separates design-level sufficiency (`S`/`E` worst-case route and capacity proof at `CP1`/`CP2`) from execution-level reconciliation (`X` original evidence of actual adherence at `CP6`).
12. **Is the P0/B5/Approval-B sequence preserved?**  
    **Yes.** Section 5 and `PO-22` preserve `P0 qualification → separately authorized Approval B → interface-tailored V2-2B → any later implementation or execution decision` categorically across all V2-2B implementation authorization and execution, and keep `B5 G1` at `HOLD`.
13. **Are CP0–CP6 non-circular?**  
    **Yes.** `CP0`–`CP6` follow a strict causal progression: criteria (`CP0`) → feasibility (`CP1`) → concrete design (`CP2`) → pre-execution bindings and `Class A` premises (`CP3`) → pre-dispatch eligibility (`CP4`) → cutoff/frontier sealing (`CP5`) → post-execution continuity and reconciliation (`CP6`).
14. **Is the register genuinely backend-neutral?**  
    **Yes.** All obligations are expressed in terms of capabilities, bindings, protection domains, enforcing-set properties, and byte/object bounds, with no unstated OS, filesystem, process, container, or codec assumptions.
