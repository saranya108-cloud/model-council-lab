## 8. Central-Question Disposition (Including Strongest Contrary Interpretation)

### The Central Question
Does Sequence 11 Revision 2 correctly conclude that **UQ-4 cannot be resolved merely by an abstract rule permitting or constraining both alternatives, because the unresolved proposition itself is the consequential M-A/M-B acquisition/custody choice**?

### Strongest Contrary Interpretation (Challenging Outcome B)
The strongest contrary argument is that UQ-4 could be resolved at the abstract governance level—analogous to Sequence 10's resolution of UQ-12—In one of two ways:
1. **Permissive/constraining abstract rule:** Governance could adopt an abstract rule stating the binding evidentiary, provenance (`AR-1`–`AR-3`), custody (`SUCC R-30`), independence (`SUCC R-24`–`R-25`), and accounting (`U12-01`–`U12-20`) invariants that any M-A or M-B realization must satisfy, while deferring the selection of M-A versus M-B to concrete route closure or Approval B authorization; or
2. **High-level policy choice as abstract architecture:** The Human Chair could choose between M-A (executor direct read) and M-B (Chair-mediated transfer without executor device access) today as a high-level architectural policy choice while leaving concrete provider names, tool implementations, PB designs, and IM classifications deferred.

### Independent Evaluation of Questions 1–6

1. **Does Revision 2 wrongly classify a legitimately abstract governance decision as concrete?**
   **No.** Under [FABLE-SEQUENCING-ARCHITECTURE-REV4.txt](file:///Users/aclab/aclab/model-council-lab/docs/governance/decision-0009/provenance/sequencing-rev4-2026-11-12/FABLE-SEQUENCING-ARCHITECTURE-REV4.txt#L83-L103) (`SEQ §F` steps 1–3), whether a provision is abstract or concrete is determined by what adopting it settles, never by its level of generality or lack of implementation names. A decision that chooses whether the executor directly reads `P/` via device access (`M-A`) or the Chair acquires and transfers the objects to a device-isolated executor (`M-B`) fixes *"the path that evidence or capabilities travel, or the arrangement of the parts that handle them"* (`SEQ §F` step 2, bullet 2) and *"identif[ies] which actual components carry the enforcing responsibilities"* (`SEQ §F` step 2, bullet 4), allowing downstream work to treat that answer to "how" as settled (`SEQ §F` step 3). Moreover, adopted `ABE AB-83` and `CLOS CR-10(4)` / `CR-10(10)` explicitly classify acquisition method and the `M-A/M-B choice (UQ-4)` as concrete envelope elements subject to `ABE AB-81` and `CLOS` closure before first commitment.

2. **Could UQ-4 be resolved by a policy-level M-A/M-B rule while leaving concrete realization open?**
   **No, not without either leaving UQ-4 unanswered or making a concrete commitment requiring prior `CLOS` closure.**
   - If the policy-level rule *permits or constrains both M-A and M-B* without choosing between them (Candidate §§9.1–9.2), it leaves the exact question asked by UQ-4 (*M-A versus M-B*) undecided. Indeed, `ABE` and `CLOS` already establish the abstract constraints governing both routes while explicitly retaining UQ-4 as unresolved (`ABE AB-41`, `§24`; `CLOS CR-10(10)`, `CR-43`).
   - If the policy-level rule *selects M-A or M-B* (or fixes a conditional rule that binds which route applies under specified conditions, Candidate §9.3), it is by definition a concrete commitment under `SEQ §F` and `ABE AB-82`, which `ABE AB-81` and `CLOS CR-108` prohibit making until a scoped `CLOS` closure showing, independent verification, and Chair closure determination (`CLOS CR-76`) have been completed for that commitment.

3. **Does the effect-based architecture make the M-A/M-B choice itself a concrete element because it fixes acquisition responsibility and the defining custody structure?**
   **Yes.** Choosing M-A vs. M-B determines who performs the initial protected read, whether the receiving executor is barred from device access, and whether an inherent Chair-to-executor custody handoff exists.
