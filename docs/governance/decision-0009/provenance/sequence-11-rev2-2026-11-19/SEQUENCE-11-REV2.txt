## 2. UQ-4 definition and closure condition

### Recovered documentary meaning

**M-A — executor direct read:** the executor directly obtains the authorized source objects through device access.

**M-B — Chair-mediated transfer:** the Chair supplies the authorized objects and Chair-computed digests to an executor without device access, introducing a custody handoff.

These definitions appear explicitly in the [original candidate’s AB-19](</Users/aclab/.codex/attachments/8f88ce07-4517-4ed8-bb9f-f6efcafd2cff/Pasted text.txt:245>). The recovered attachment is 51,974 bytes, SHA-256:

This establishes the alternatives’ documentary meaning without adopting the original draft’s procedural assumptions, sufficiency claims, timing or preference.

**U4-I01 — SATISFIED for documentary label definition and provenance.**

### What UQ-4 asks

UQ-4 asks which acquisition arrangement will govern: executor direct acquisition or Chair acquisition/supply to an executor without device access.

Its subject is therefore a consequential allocation of acquisition responsibility and an associated custody relationship. It is not merely a request to state conditions applicable to every acquisition route.

ABE §24 requires resolution **before concrete commitment of the route**, including its first fixation in an authorization. CLOS CR-43 preserves that deadline.

### Scope distinctions

- **Acquisition route** identifies how the authorized source objects are first obtained and by whom. This is central to UQ-4.
- **Transfer route** concerns subsequent conveyance. M-B necessarily introduces the identified Chair-to-executor handoff; M-A does not eliminate other transfers.
- **Custody arrangement** concerns holders, effective control and the evidentiary basis for continuity and retrieval. Both alternatives require one.
- **Custody transitions** are changes in holders or control. A design commitment does not prove they occurred.
- **Receiving-domain transition** is entry into another governed evidence/accounting domain. It is not synonymous with every custody handoff.
- **Provider identity and intermediary involvement** are separate concrete matters unless the choice fixes or relies on them.
- **Possession** does not establish exclusive control. **Control** concerns effective capabilities, including capabilities shared within a protection domain.
- **Protected access, evidence production, evidence transfer and retention** remain separately governed acts and obligations. Choosing an alternative authorizes none of them.

### Closure versus resolution

A non-governing proposal can identify an answer without selecting it. Applicable concrete closure makes an exact item eligible for commitment. Only the Chair’s consequential decision settles the choice.

Thus:

- Closing an M-A candidate alone does not select M-A.
- Closing an M-B candidate alone does not select M-B.
- Closing both does not resolve UQ-4.
- Declaring both potentially permissible does not resolve UQ-4.

Closure and commitment may be recorded on one occasion as distinct acts. No extra meeting or new gate is introduced. The choice must be settled no later than the act that first commits the affected route; it cannot be postponed until after protected exposure.

## 3. M-A, M-B and bounded comparison

### 3.1 M-A — executor direct read

**Responsibility, access and visibility.** M-A places direct acquisition responsibility with the executor’s device-access path. The proposed design must identify how that executor relates to ABE’s acquisition component, tools and plaintext recipients. “Executor” does not automatically mean one process, one protection domain or universal plaintext visibility.

The AC acquires the complete authorized bytes as required by ABE. Other actors receive only their authorized visibility scope. Read-only source access does not establish absence of output production elsewhere.

**Source provenance and PB.** Direct access to the named path does not establish:

- AR-1 source binding;
- AR-2 completeness;
- AR-3 acquisition coherence.

The needed relationships require credible, lawful, independently reviewable proof bases. Direct acquisition does not make the executor’s account independent. Later stability and nonreplacement claims remain separately required only where relied upon.

**IM.** No particular metadata method follows from M-A. Any proposed source-identification or IM act must receive its method-specific authority classification, with sufficiency assessed separately. Read-only operation, proximity to the source or convenience does not establish permission.

**Production and custody.** “Direct” does not establish absence of copies, buffers, intermediaries, framework outputs, caches, diagnostics, provider processing or retained artifacts. Relevant routes require declared treatment or evidenced exclusions. A caller-supplied inventory cannot prove completeness.

The custody basis must cover the acquired material and relied-on support through the relevant interval. A pathname or matching digest cannot alone establish that chain.

**Independence and UQ-9.** Source, production, transfer, receiver and accounting claims must identify the producer being evaluated and the independent basis. Logical role separation does not establish capability separation. UQ-9 becomes a predecessor where the PB/custody design relies on a particular verifier-independence arrangement.

**B5 and P0.** M-A creates no universal B5 or P0 factual dependency. A claim that autonomous agents cannot copy, alter, expose or remove material may create a B5 dependency. A relied-on actual Q-parent proposition creates its specific P0 dependency. Neither is established merely by direct device access.

**Concrete U12 consequences.** The eventual showing must cover governed acquisition-side, executor-side, tool, receiver and support production, including relevant failure and terminal paths. The absence of the defining M-B handoff does not prove a smaller population or lower allocation.

**Failure, adverse outcomes and retention.** Wrong-source, partial, incoherent, unauthorized-exposure, unbounded-production and custody failures retain their applicable status and stop consequences. No retry or fallback arises from failure. Retention follows the established design; completion or stopping grants no cleanup or release authority.

**Facts needed before legitimate selection.** The proposed commitment must identify what direct-access arrangement it settles and supply the facts necessary to judge that commitment lawful, credible and sufficient. These include its acquisition responsibility, relevant access boundaries, material production/custody assumptions, independent grounding and reliance on other elements. A device or provider identity is needed at this stage if the conformance argument depends on its properties—not simply because the eventual implementation will have a name.

### 3.2 M-B — Chair-mediated transfer

**The Chair’s acquisition act.** M-B places source acquisition/supply with the Chair and denies device access to the receiving executor. The first protected read, digest or copy by the Chair is a governed act and can begin the Approval B event. It cannot be treated as ungoverned preparation for a later executor-only event.

**Chair-computed digests.** The digests are producer accounts. They may participate in integrity comparisons under stated assumptions, but do not independently establish source identity, completeness, coherence or custody history.

The Human Chair’s decision authority does not make the Chair’s produced evidence independent.

**Custody handoff and transfer provenance.** The design must account for acquisition by the Chair, emitted material, the handoff, received material, transformations and retention. Receipt, validation, acceptance for a use and admission remain distinct.

Equality between supplied and received bytes does not prove that the supplied bytes came from the authorized source or constituted its complete coherent content.

**Executor lack of device access.** This is a substantive characteristic of M-B, not an inference from the executor’s role name. Its design basis must be supported wherever the selection relies on it. Required actual-environment facts receive their proper CLOS timing classification.

Lack of device access removes the executor’s direct source-access role as defined by this alternative. It does not remove exposure through supplied plaintext, recipient-side production, provider processing or custody obligations.

**Source provenance, PB and IM.** The original-acquisition relationships remain necessary. They must be supported at the acquisition stage and preserved through transfer. A receiver cannot repair missing original evidence by recomputing the Chair’s digest.

No PB or IM method is selected. Chair-performed source-identification acts remain subject to the same authority and sufficiency tests; human performance grants no exemption.

**Production and custody.** Relevant Chair tools, source copies, digest production, transfer representations, intermediaries, receiver copies, transformations and support must be declared and bounded where governed. No assumption is made about exact artifact counts or whether M-B produces more total material than a particular M-A realization.

**Independence and UQ-9.** Both the Chair’s acquisition account and the executor’s receipt/production account must be assessed against the claims they support. Agreement between them is not automatically independent evidence. UQ-9 becomes necessary for the particular PB/custody arrangements relying on design-internal verifier roles.

**B5 and P0.** Chair mediation changes neither gate. B5 becomes relevant where source-side, transfer-side or retention claims rely on agent characterization or exclusion. Actual Q-parent reliance retains its P0 requirements. Moving an act to the Chair does not change its evidence or authority classification.

**Concrete U12 consequences.** Required support for Chair acquisition, digesting, handoff, receiver production and associated reconciliation/finalization belongs within the applicable accounting boundaries. Calling the handoff a manual action does not exempt its governed production. A receiving-domain transition, if present, adds that domain’s separate admission and accounting obligations.

**Failure, adverse outcomes and retention.** Incomplete transfer, mismatch, omission, substitution, uncertain receipt, unexpected production and loss of custody support must remain distinguishable. Failure grants no automatic resend, recapture, replacement representation or direct-read fallback. Relevant evidence remains subject to established retention, including adverse and uncertain outcomes.

**Facts needed before legitimate selection.** The commitment must specify the Chair’s acquisition/supply responsibility, the executor’s lack-of-device-access boundary, the material custody handoff and the supported basis for their conformance. Any transfer, protection, source-provenance or independence fact necessary to judge that choice must already be established at its required stage. Neither an identified Chair nor a planned digest comparison supplies that showing.

## 9. Abstract-resolution test

### 9.1 Would permitting either alternative subject to existing requirements resolve UQ-4?

**No.** It would preserve two possible answers. The recovered question asks which acquisition method will govern.

Such permission would also not establish that either alternative has a compliant concrete realization.

### 9.2 Would specifying mandatory characteristics for both resolve UQ-4?

**No.** Bounded production, lawful access, suitable recipients, independent support and custody protection constrain both alternatives. They do not decide who acquires and supplies the objects.

ABE and CLOS already impose such obligations while expressly retaining UQ-4 as unresolved. Repeating or supplementing them cannot be represented as settling the choice.

### 9.3 Could an abstract conditional rule select between them without becoming a concrete route commitment?

A genuinely abstract rule can state criteria for a future choice. If application of those criteria and the consequential choice remain open, UQ-4 remains open.

A rule that fixes which acquisition responsibility applies under specified conditions can instead settle a contingent acquisition architecture. Its classification depends on its substantive effect, not its lack of provider names. If later work may rely on that architecture as settled, applicable concrete closure is required.

Neither a conditional label nor a deferred runtime branch supplies an exemption. A policy that delegates or automates later selection would require its own authority and analysis; none is proposed here.

### 9.4 Does the wording and lineage make the choice itself unresolved?

**Yes.** The defining text identifies two acquisition methods. The successors carry that same choice forward, and the adopted texts require it to be resolved before route commitment.

Recovery removes Revision 1’s uncertainty about whether the labels might denote some different, purely classificatory policy question.

This conclusion does not depend solely on the original draft. Current ABE AB-41/§24 and CLOS CR-10/43 preserve the choice and its governing consequence.

### 9.5 Can a genuine governance-level distinction still be resolved separately?

**Yes, but it would answer a different proposition.**

Governance may separately settle general evidentiary duties, authority boundaries, closure standards or selection criteria without settling implementation. Existing architecture already does much of this.

A high-level M-A/M-B choice can also leave implementation details open. However, choosing who directly acquires and who receives through a Chair handoff fixes substantive acquisition/custody structure. It is not abstract merely because it is described at a governance level or leaves a provider unnamed.

### 9.6 What may remain open after the choice?

A detail may remain open only if the exact commitment neither fixes it nor relies on treating it as settled, and its applicable deadline remains respected.

Potential residue includes a particular provider, component realization, transfer implementation, actor binding, representation or accounting implementation. These are not automatically deferrable: any such detail becomes required earlier when its properties are necessary to judge the proposed commitment lawful, credible or sufficient.

The following cannot remain unspecified where material to the choice:

- who acquires and supplies the source objects;
- the access/custody distinction being committed;
- the requirements reaching that commitment;
- its necessary independent grounding and dependencies;
- design-validity premises;
- the legitimate boundary between settled matters and later decisions.

Execution outcomes remain unestablished before execution. A valid design may carry execution-observable premises only under the adopted conditions.

### Result of the repeated test

The recovered definitions strengthen the finding that UQ-4’s unresolved proposition is an acquisition-method choice. They remove the missing-definition premise and the alternative interpretation associated with it.

They do not establish that either choice is currently eligible for commitment. Nor do they establish that every implementation detail must precede the choice.

UQ-12 remains materially different: its adopted resolution answers which accounting regime and governing allocation rule apply while expressly preserving concrete realization. A rule allowing both acquisition alternatives would leave UQ-4’s named question unanswered.
