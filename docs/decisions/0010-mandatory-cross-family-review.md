## Context

[Decision 0002](0002-mvp-experiment-design.md) prohibits a Solver from approving its own work and separates authoring, verification, and revision roles. It does not explicitly prevent same-family approval after roles rotate, nor does it distinguish the independent-review standard for operational acceptance from the deliberately homogeneous research conditions.

The Human Chair has affirmed that no model may independently certify its own authored revision. The original requirement for a reviewer from a family different from every substantive contributor is replaced by revision-scoped independence: two families may collaborate repeatedly without historical contributions permanently excluding either family from future review. Genuine independent challenge remains required at formal adoption and qualification gates.

## Binding Decision

**No model may provide the qualifying review of substantive text it wrote. Findings, objections, recommendations, and suggested language do not by themselves make a reviewer a co-author; the author who decides whether and how to incorporate them owns the resulting revision. A reviewer cannot independently certify its own substantive wording merely by reviewing the completed document.**

This rule applies to operational council review of code, patches, governance changes, technical proposals, security assessments, reports, and other artifacts offered as independently reviewed evidence for acceptance.

1. **Authorship and influence.** The author is the model that writes and revises the artifact and is accountable for each revision. A reviewer's findings, objections, recommendations, and suggested language are inputs the author may accept, modify, or reject. Influence on a revision, including the author's correction of defects the reviewer identified, does not make the reviewer a co-author and does not disqualify the reviewer from reviewing the resulting revision. Reviewer-authored language that the author adopts verbatim is governed by clause 2.
2. **Verbatim adoption of reviewer language.** Where the author incorporates substantive reviewer-authored language verbatim, or with only non-substantive edits:
   - (a) the author must identify the adopted language in the findings record and independently assess its correctness;
   - (b) for Routine work, this documented cross-check is sufficient;
   - (c) for Formal Adoption/Qualification, High-Risk work, or any safety-critical conclusion, the material reviewer-authored language must either (i) be substantively rewritten by the author and then independently evaluated by an eligible reviewer, who may be the reviewer that suggested the original language, or (ii) receive an additional independent check by an eligible reviewer who is neither the writer of that language nor the artifact's author;
   - (d) a reviewer's review of the completed document does not constitute independent certification of the reviewer's own substantive wording;
   - (e) the author identifies whether adopted language is substantive and whether a conclusion is safety-critical; the reviewer may challenge that classification, and the Human Chair resolves any disagreement. Until resolved, the language is treated as substantive and the conclusion as safety-critical.
3. **Findings record.** Each finding receives an identifier, a severity (blocking or non-blocking), and a disposition: open, resolved, accepted as note, rejected with rationale, disputed, or withdrawn by the reviewer. For each revision, the author records a response to every open finding. Only the reviewer who raised a blocking finding may mark it resolved or withdrawn. The author's rejection of a blocking finding makes it disputed, not resolved.
4. **Review rounds.** A review round consists of one author revision, pinned to an exact identifier (commit or content hash), and the reviewer's verdict on that revision. The reviewer reviews the complete revised artifact, not merely the changed passages, and states the scope reviewed. Where review scope is narrowed, the material, changes, and contributor scope must be explicit, and the review cannot be represented as approval of unreviewed portions. Material changes after an ACCEPT require renewed review of the resulting revision.
5. **Consensus.** Consensus requires an explicit ACCEPT (with or without non-blocking notes) on the exact pinned revision, zero unresolved blocking findings, and satisfaction of clause 2 and the requirements of the applicable tier. A rejected or disputed blocking finding is never consensus. Consensus establishes qualifying review; it is not adoption, which remains with the Human Chair.
6. **Review convergence and Human Chair notification.** After three review rounds with unresolved blocking findings, and after every three additional review rounds in which blocking findings remain unresolved, the Human Chair must be notified of the outstanding issues, each model's position, and the progress made toward resolution. Notification does not automatically suspend the review or require a third model family. The author and reviewer may continue working toward consensus unless the Human Chair directs escalation, modification, or termination. A rejected blocking finding must be documented and presented to the Human Chair as a dispute, whether or not any notification threshold has been reached.
7. **Governance tiers.** The Human Chair assigns the tier and may change it at any time. Additional model families are not required for ordinary revision cycles at any tier unless the Human Chair directs.
   - **Unassigned work** proceeds provisionally under Routine requirements. Provisional Routine status does not waive higher-tier requirements: work offered for formal adoption, qualification, or a safety-critical conclusion cannot be accepted on provisional status; if the Human Chair assigns Formal Adoption/Qualification or High-Risk, the work is not qualified at that tier until that tier's requirements are satisfied for the pinned revision; and provisionally reviewed work must not be represented as qualified at any higher tier.
   - **Routine:** the reviewer is not the author; clauses 1–6 apply; the record is the findings record and the verdict.
   - **Formal Adoption/Qualification:** Routine requirements, plus at least one genuinely independent qualifying reviewer and the full record under clause 9. A reviewer from a different underlying foundation-model family than the author is preferred, and is required when the Human Chair directs or when needed for genuine independent challenge. Any cross-family claim must be verified under clause 8.
   - **High-Risk:** Formal Adoption/Qualification requirements, plus the Human Chair may require cross-family review and may require a fresh third-family reviewer outside the author–reviewer cycle. That reviewer must not have written the revision under review or the language being qualified; historical contribution elsewhere in the lineage alone is not disqualifying.
8. **Verified model-family independence.** Where cross-family review is required, distinct agents, instances, model versions, sizes, fine-tunes, providers, or personas do not suffice if they share the same underlying foundation-model family or lineage. A different provider label alone does not establish independence. Family identity must be supported by model-identity evidence; unknown or ambiguous lineage does not qualify as cross-family review.
9. **Provenance and disposition record.** For Formal Adoption/Qualification and High-Risk work, the council record must identify the reviewed artifact and pinned revision, the author, the reviewer or reviewers, their observed or verified model identities and underlying families, any verbatim-adopted reviewer language and how clause 2 was satisfied, the scope and purpose of review, the tier and whether it was provisional, the eligibility rationale, the findings record, and the disposition. At every tier, self-review, same-family review, and unknown-family review must be labeled accurately and never presented as qualifying independent or cross-family approval unless they satisfy the applicable requirements.
10. **Fail closed.** If no reviewer satisfies the applicable tier's requirements, or consensus has not been reached, independent review remains **PENDING / NOT QUALIFIED**; the work cannot be marked as independently approved. Escalate availability or identity problems to the Human Chair without silently waiving or relabeling the requirement.
11. **Human Chair authority.** The Human Chair assigns tiers, has final authority over disputed findings, may require additional independent review (including cross-family or fresh-family review) at any tier, and retains all consequential-action and final-acceptance authority under Decision 0002. Chair authorization is not evidence that the required independent review happened and does not turn a model's certification of its own text into qualifying independent review.
12. **Relationship to Decision 0002's reviewer-family statement.** From the Human Chair's explicit adoption of this amendment on 2026-11-08, and prospectively only, this decision governs reviewer-family eligibility for operational council review of artifacts within its scope. To the extent that Decision 0002's statement requiring an independent reviewer from a foundation-model family different from every substantive model author conflicts with this decision, this decision supersedes that statement for such review, and only to the extent of that conflict. This provision does not amend the text of Decision 0002; does not affect Decision 0002's experimental design, Condition B/C/D definitions, role prohibitions, Human Chair authority, or other independent-review safeguards; does not weaken the no-self-certification rule; does not remove cross-family review where clause 7 or the Human Chair requires it; and does not create any automatic third-family requirement. Reviews completed before adoption retain the classification determined under the governance in effect when they were completed.

## Experimental Controls Are Preserved

This governance requirement does **not** cancel or retroactively relabel the M1 experimental conditions in Decision 0002.

- **Condition B** intentionally studies same-agent self-refinement. Its self-review is an experimental step, **not** qualifying operational independent review.
- **Condition C** intentionally studies isolated, homogeneous instances with role-separated verification. Its experimental "Independent Verifier" label refers to role/context separation, **not** qualification under this operational reviewer-independence rule.
- **Condition D** studies heterogeneous councils with counterbalanced role assignments. A D review meets the operational standard only where actual author/reviewer identities, relevant contributions to the current revision and changes under review, and the gate's required independence level satisfy this decision.
- An **External Evaluator** assesses objective experimental outcomes; its evaluation does not silently replace a required operational independent model review, including cross-family review where required for the gate.

Research results must retain their intended experimental classification even where they do not meet the operational acceptance standard.

## Enforcement and Evidence Boundary

Effective prospectively from the Human Chair's explicit adoption of this amendment on 2026-11-08, reviewer eligibility must be checked against the applicable tier's requirements. The check must consider the current pinned revision and the review scope and purpose, and must be made at initial review assignment, whenever the revision, scope, roles, author, or tier change, and before a claim of independent approval is accepted. Where the current harness lacks automated checks, the council must apply the rule procedurally and record the resulting evidence and any unqualified disposition.

This decision is **documentation-only**. It does not authorize modification of the MCL runner, tests, configuration, historical records, live execution, or external systems. Future code-level enforcement, migration, and verification require a separately scoped and authorized implementation.
